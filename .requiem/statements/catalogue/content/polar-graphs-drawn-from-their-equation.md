A drawn polar graph (parametric-polar's Polar Graphs) is sampled from its own equation over a full turn and drawn whole, so the curve is the student's copy of that equation; its four options cross two slips that each change the picture -- cos and sin traded, which turns the curve, against n doubled for a rose (twice the petals), the sign of b turned for a limaçon or cardioid (it faces the other way), or a negated for a circle (the far side of the origin) -- so every option is a different drawing and the key is never the odd one out. The text names the curve's r at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, which only the key matches, and the checker holds every drawn point to that equation and the whole curve to the drawing. Petal counts, the farthest r and a curve's name are asked without a picture, since the picture would answer them.
