e is met before ln is used: exponential functions gains e and Continuous Growth (before All Together, as its ninth level, with a longerBecause), and logarithms' Evaluate -- the first level to say ln is the log to base e -- depends on it, as does Solve Exponential Equations, whose continuous stories take ln of e to a power. e arrives as what compound interest tends to when it is compounded ever more often -- $1 at 100% compounded n times a year is (1 + 1/n)ⁿ, which climbs to 2.71828... and never passes it -- so it follows Compound Interest's own model rather than arriving as a bare constant. The level then compounds n times a year, P(1 + r/n)^(nt), and continuously, P·e^(rt), reads a continuous rate off a·e^(kt), the factor e^k it multiplies by each step (6% continuous is 6.18% a year), and the model's value at a time. It is the one exponential level that rounds, since e makes every value irrational, and each question names its places. Evaluate also widens its ln and common-log rows, a root of a power of e or 10 being a fraction power (ln ∛(e²) = 2/3, log √1000 = 3/2), so neither is a token few among the log_b rows.
