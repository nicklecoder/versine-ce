Conics asks for foci, vertices and centers as exact points -- two of them in the points box, judged as a set, one in the pair box -- so the ellipses and hyperbolas whose foci are asked are built on Pythagorean triples (5, 4, 3 to 13, 12, 5), which make c whole; a focus at 3 + √7 could only be a choice or a decimal, and a level of exact points would then tell the student which rows are whole. Lengths, slopes and the number in a directrix go in the fraction box, which says nothing of whether the number is whole. The foci are never visible on the curve, so those rows carry the graph, revealing the foci and vertices once answered; rows asking a vertex, a co-vertex or an axis length carry none, since the vertices would be read straight off a drawing on whole-number grid points (play/visuals/hold-hides-too-good-pictures).
