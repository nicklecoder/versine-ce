Every numeric answer of one question shape goes in the same kind of answer box, so the box never tells the student what kind of number the answer is. In unit-circle and identities L1 a rational value (4/3) and an irrational one (√7/4) were keyed as frac and roots, and the frac box against the roots box gave the answer's form away before any working; now every number on those levels is a roots answer, which accepts plain fractions and decimals like 2.5 exactly (4/3, −4/3, 8/6 all parse). The rule is about leaking the answer, not about uniform screens: two question shapes on one level, or in All Together, may use different boxes when the question text already states the answer's form ("in degrees", "to 1 decimal place", "which interval"), because then the box tells the student nothing the question has not. Dropping a shape or leaving a level out of All Together only to keep one box is not what this asks. A choice question is a different kind of question and keeps its options.
