The units skill keeps a minority of choice rows on each level (roughly a fifth) that build a feel for size rather than a procedure: which unit fits a familiar thing (a pencil is 7.5 in, not 7.5 ft) and which of two amounts is more (1 kg or 1 lb, 1 m² or 1,000 cm²). Conversion fluency without a sense of size lets a student accept an answer a thousand times off. The rows stay inside the levels rather than forming a level of their own, so the feel is practised in each system as it is taught. Every decoy is truthful and at least three times off the right size, so only one choice is fair, and the checker keeps its own table of typical sizes and asserts that margin.
