Rectangular to Polar asks a point at a special angle in exact polar form, (−1, √3) as (2, 2π/3), with θ in radians half the time, chosen from four options crossing two slips: r left as x² + y² without the root, and θ half a turn out, since the inverse tangent cannot tell (x, y) from (−x, −y). Points are built from r = 2k at the 30° family and r = k√2 at the 45° family, so x and y are k or k√3 and nothing is over 2. The level's typed r and rounded θ are calculator work; this is the unit circle run backwards, which calculus uses.
