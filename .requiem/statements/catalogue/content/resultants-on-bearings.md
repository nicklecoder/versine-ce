A quarter of vectors' Resultants is on bearings, clockwise from north and written 060°: a ship's two legs or a plane's heading and a wind, asking the distance or resultant speed, or the bearing of the resultant, to 1 decimal place, and saying it is measured clockwise from north (catalogue/content/direction-angle-stated). A bearing b is r sin b east and r cos b north. Navigation is where resultants are used, and bearings are taught in trig ratios' Elevation & Bearings, on which the level depends. 'Speed over the ground', which is wrong on a river, is now 'resultant speed'.
