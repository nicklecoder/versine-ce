Rectangular to Polar asks which polar coordinates name the same point as a given (r, θ): a negative r half a turn round (−r, θ ± 180°) or the same r a whole turn round (r, θ ± 360°), in degrees or radians. The four options cross two slips -- r's sign turned with θ left alone (the point through the origin) and θ measured clockwise (the point reflected in the x-axis) -- and the angle is never on an axis, where those coincide. Unlike rectangular coordinates, a polar point has endless names, and a student who knows only one cannot read r = 2 sin θ passing through (−1, 7π/6).
