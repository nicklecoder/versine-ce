The Binomial Theorem's numbers stay at hand-arithmetic size, since each answer is a product a student multiplies out under a par time: A Given Term's coefficient at most 5,000 (it once asked for −393,216 from (2x − 4)⁸), Full Expansion's coefficients at most 2,500 with the numbers inside the binomial kept small (a 2 or 3, b up to ±3) at the fifth and sixth powers, and Binomial Probability's denominator at most 8,000 (the die's 6⁵ = 7,776 stays; 5⁶ = 15,625, as in 15,561/15,625, goes). Full Expansion reaches n = 5 and 6 for every kind, rather than being three-quarters cubes and fourth powers, and writes each term worked out (10(2x)³(−3)² = 10 × 8 × 9x³ = 720x³) instead of 'working each out gives'.
