An explanation of a system shows each step a student is graded on, in the order they write it: the equation with one letter put in as it stands before anything is simplified (x² + (−2x + 10)² = 25, or ((3x − 5)/2 + 2)² written out before it becomes ((3x − 1)/2)²), a fraction cleared by naming what both sides are multiplied by, the squares multiplied out, the quadratic brought to one side, and a common factor divided out aloud (divided by 5); in elimination, every combination named with the equation it gives, 4 × the second − the first gives −4y + 6z = 0, which divided by 2 is A: −2y + 3z = 0, then the pair named A and B and combined the same way, and each letter found by putting the ones before it back in, x + 2(−1) − 3(3) = 4. systems-2's Line Meets Circle once said only multiplied out and brought to one side, and Three Variables wrote the pair already halved and named no combination for the second elimination, so a student checking their own working could not find where it went wrong. Counting where a line meets a circle is explained the way the level teaches it, by putting the line in and taking the discriminant of what is left, not by a distance formula the course has not met.
