An identities choice question takes its wrong options first from the slips that expression invites, listed per template: 2 sin θ or 2 sin 2θ for sin 2θ, sin 4θ for 4 sin θ cos θ, 1 + 2 sin²θ for cos 2θ, the cofunction or reciprocal swapped in, a dropped square, a sign flipped. Only then the answer's negative and the general pool. Random filler (sin 6θ offered for 1 − 2 sin²θ) teaches nothing, because no one would pick it. The exactly-one-equal guarantee is unchanged: a listed slip that happens to equal the answer is skipped.
