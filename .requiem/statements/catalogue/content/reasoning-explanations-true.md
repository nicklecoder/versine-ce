Every sentence of a reasoning explanation must be true as written, not only its conclusion, because a learner copies the general claims as rules. A review found "the converse and the contrapositive can differ", which is false: a statement and its contrapositive always agree. The aside now names only forms that can differ from the statement (the inverse, and a sentence with one part negated, which are the decoys the equivalent-forms choice offers since its decoys cross two slips), and the equivalent-forms level also asks which form follows from a known one, naming how each form is made from the other.
