Questions that name a quadrilateral (which special quadrilateral a list of facts proves, or what a shape on the grid is) are built so the inclusive and exclusive definitions of trapezoid and kite give the same answer. US textbooks disagree on whether a parallelogram is a trapezoid and a rhombus a kite, and a student taught either way should not be marked wrong. So a trapezoid answer always has exactly one pair of parallel sides, a kite answer never has all four sides equal, and the answer is always the most specific name the facts force, with 'none of these is certain' when they force none. The checker names shapes by its own closure over the theorems, and by exact slopes and squared lengths on the grid.
