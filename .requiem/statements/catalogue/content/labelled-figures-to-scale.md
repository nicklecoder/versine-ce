A figure whose sides carry numbers is drawn from those numbers: a triangle labelled 10, 21 and 25 is built from its three side lengths (the quadrilaterals midpoint-triangle rows, as the congruence and similarity figures already are), so the longest label sits on the longest side and the ratios match. A picture that contradicts its own numbers teaches a student to distrust figures, or to trust the wrong one. Shapes too thin to read (smallest angle under about 22 degrees) are skipped rather than drawn distorted.
