rational-expr's Unlike Denominators has denominators that share a factor (1/(x² − 4) + 1/(x + 2), 3/((x + 1)(x + 2)) − 2/((x + 1)(x − 3))) as well as ones that do not, and numerators in x, so the common denominator is built from the factors -- each as often as any one denominator has it -- and is not always the product of the two; the blurb says so. Multiply & Divide divides by a fraction whose denominator differs from the first's, so flipping it is what makes the factors cancel, and multiplies fractions whose cross-cancelling factors are inside quadratics. Complex Fractions mixes (a + b/x)/(c + d/x), 1/x² terms and a 1/(x + c) inside with the (1 ± a/x)/(1 ± b/x) shape that was nearly all of it.
