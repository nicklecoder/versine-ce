The Sample Spaces level mostly asks learners to list or recognise outcomes rather than count them: which list is the whole sample space, and which outcome is missing from a partial list. Counting by the multiplication principle stays as a smaller share. This follows 7.SP.8b, which asks students to represent sample spaces by lists, tables and tree diagrams; counting alone let a learner skip the listing. Spaces are kept to at most 9 outcomes so a list fits a phone, and decoy lists are truthful slips: one order only (HT kept, TH dropped), only the outcomes that start with the first step's first outcome, and one outcome written twice in place of the next, crossed as decoys-cross-two-slips describes (the order slip, the repeat, and both). A decoy list with one outcome left out is not offered, because the whole list would then be the longest option every time; the missing-outcome question asks for that slip instead.
