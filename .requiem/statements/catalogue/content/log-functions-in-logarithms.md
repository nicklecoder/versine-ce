The level on log functions -- y = log_b x as the inverse of y = bˣ and its reflection in y = x, its key points (1, 0) and (b, 1), its domain, range, x-intercept and vertical asymptote, rising or falling by its base, its reflections and stretches, matching a graph to its equation, and the inverse of an exponential or a log -- is a level of logarithms, Log Functions before All Together, depending on inverses' Graph Reflection; logarithms' own Log as Exponent comes before it in the skill's order, so needs no edge. US Algebra 2 and precalculus courses teach inverse functions first and then introduce the log as the inverse of bˣ, so inverses must not wait for logarithms: with the level in inverses, the whole inverses skill opened only after logarithms, the reverse of that order, and a student reached the reflection in y = x only after solving log equations. In logarithms the level also sits after ln & Change of Base, which its stretch rows use (log₄ x is log₂ x times 1/2). Inverses no longer declares logarithms; logarithms declares inverses, and nothing either skill builds on reaches logarithms, so the graph stays acyclic.
