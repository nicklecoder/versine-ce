trig-ratios' Complementary sin/cos keeps the solve-for-x rows, sin(2x + 10)° = cos(3x + 5)°, to about four in ten, so the level practises the cofunction fact (an angle's sine is its complement's cosine, a ratio read from the other acute angle) more than linear algebra. The value rows use any ratio, not only a Pythagorean triple's, which gives them room to fill the library. The stem never states the fact it tests ('sin and cos of complementary angles are equal').
