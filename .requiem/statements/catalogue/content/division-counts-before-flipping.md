Fraction division is explained by counting before it is explained by flipping. How Many Fit (frac-muldiv) explains each problem the way its fits picture works: restate the dividend in the divisor's pieces and count the copies ('In 8ths, 3/4 is 6/8, which is 6 copies of 1/8. So 6 fit'). Flip-the-second-and-multiply arrives only from Dividing Fractions on, where the divisor is general and counting stops being doable in your head, and there it is written out as a shortcut for that count, with the unreduced product shown before it is simplified. Teaching the flip first makes it a spell with no reason behind it; the counting level exists so the shortcut has something to be a shortcut for.
