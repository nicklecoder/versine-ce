Every radical-eq prompt says the answer may be none: 'Solve … . Give every solution, or say there is none.', as trig-equations words it. Between a tenth and a third of each level's rows have no solution, and a bare 'Solve:' told the student there was something to find, which made 'no solution' read as giving up rather than as an answer. The solutions box reads 'none' and 'no solution' as the empty set.
