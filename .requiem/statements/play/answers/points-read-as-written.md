The points box reads the other ways a student writes the same points rather than refusing them: they don't meet, do not meet, never intersect, no intersection, no points of intersection, DNE and does not exist as none; a period after the points, as at the end of a sentence; the set in braces, {(1, 2), (3, 4)}; spaces after a sign or around a fraction's slash, ( - 1/2 , 5/2 ); one point said letter by letter in the question's order, x = 2, y = −1, z = 3 (x = then y = then z =, so the order is the coordinates' and never a guess); and a dollar sign on a price, ($4.50, $1, $0.75). Each was refused with a hint that blamed the form, so a right answer cost an attempt and taught nothing about the mathematics. Letters in another order (y = 4, x = 3), or other letters, are still refused with a hint saying the order, since reading them would be a guess at which coordinate is which. Every hint says parentheses, the US word, not brackets (catalogue/content/us-notation).
