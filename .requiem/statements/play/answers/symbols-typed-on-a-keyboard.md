Every symbol an answer box reads has a spelling on a standard US QWERTY keyboard, and the box shows that spelling before the student needs it, not only in a refusal: a small key guide above every free-text box lists the stand-ins its type reads (<= for ≤, >= for ≥, != for ≠, +- for ±, inf for ∞, sqrt(5) for √5, x^2 for x², root(3, x) for ∛x, pi for π), and the live preview draws the real symbol as it is typed, so the student sees the stand-in become the notation. Students practise on ordinary keyboards and phones, where none of ≠, ≤, ≥, ±, √ or ∞ is a key, and a spelling discovered only by being refused costs an attempt and teaches guessing. The guide is the same for every problem of a type, so it says nothing about the answer (principles/no-answer-before-commit). The common alternatives people reach for are read too (=/=, /= and <> for ≠; =< and => for ≤ and ≥; +/- for ±; infinity and U for ∞ and ∪), while the guide teaches one.
